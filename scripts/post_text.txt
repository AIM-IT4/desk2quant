A candidate gave an answer in an options interview last week that sounded mathematically spotless:

"If we're delta-hedged, our directional risk is zero. A 4% move in spot doesn't hurt us."

On a trading desk, that candidate just blew up the book.

Why? Because Black-Scholes assumes continuous replication. Desks rehedge discretely.

The actual instantaneous PnL on a delta-hedged book isn't zero:

PnL ~ 0.5 * Gamma * (dS)^2 + Theta * dt

If you are short gamma, that 4% move produces a quadratic loss that steamrolls your accrued theta. If you don't quote your gamma and your local vol smile dynamic in the first 10 seconds of that question, the desk knows you've only read textbook pricing proofs.
