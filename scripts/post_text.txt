In textbook quantitative finance:
"Delta = 0 means directional risk is eliminated."

On a live options desk:
Delta = 0 with short gamma is how books get liquidated in 3 minutes.

Here is why:

Black-Scholes assumes continuous replication. But desks rehedge discretely.

In the discrete world, your instantaneous PnL is not zero:

PnL ~= 0.5 * Gamma * (dS)^2 + Theta * dt

If you are short gamma, a 4% spot shock produces a quadratic loss that steamrolls your accrued theta. If you do not quote your discrete gamma exposure and local vol smile dynamics in the first 10 seconds of that question, the desk knows you have only read textbook derivations.

This is just Trap #1.

In trading desk and market maker interviews, interviewers test 3 deeper model-risk failure modes that break candidates with spotless GPAs:

- The Flat Vega Fallacy: Why parallel vol shifts ignore Vanna and Volga during skew rotation.
- The PnL Attribution Disconnect: Realized variance scalping vs implied carry slippage.
- The Dupire Breakdown: Why pure local vol matches terminal prices but produces flat forward smiles (and why desks run SLV).
